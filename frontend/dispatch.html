How the dispatch system works

From "something is wrong" to a technician at the door

Nothing here is a black box. Every step is a rule you can read, every number is shown on the screen above, and every decision is written to the log of the request it belongs to.

Someone reports a problem

A facility manager reports it in the app or on this page: which site, which unit, what is wrong, how urgent. The system checks that the unit belongs to that site, then opens the request: a beacon appears on the map.

It is ranked against every other open request

Each request gets a priority score out of 100: severity (urgent 40, high 30, normal 15), how critical the site is (a hospital or data centre +20, an office or hotel +10, a residence +5), how long it has waited (the score rises while nobody takes it), and how likely this unit is to fail again, from the memory. The reasons are shown with every request.

The search is layered: small first, wider only when needed

Like a ride-hailing app, the first layer is everyone free within 3 km. If nobody is free inside, the system skips to 8 km, then 15 km, then 30 km. The rings on the map are these layers; the amber one is the layer being notified right now.

Only technicians who are online and inside the layer see it

Like a ride-hailing captain, a technician switches Online or Offline in the app. Offline technicians receive nothing and are never asked, not even as a last resort. Online technicians outside the current layer do not see the request either: an offer reaches a phone only when that technician is inside the radius being notified.

Demo mode and Manual mode

In Demo mode the simulated technicians answer by themselves, some accept and some decline, so the whole story plays without anyone touching anything. In Manual mode nobody answers until a person decides: either you, with the Accept and Reject buttons on this screen, or a real technician on the phone app. The same two modes exist in the app: a technician's phone can be set to Demo (the simulation plays that technician) or Manual (the person decides), and a reporter chooses the mode of each request.

Everyone in the layer is notified at once

A notification goes to all free technicians inside the current layer at the same moment. Each one sees the job, the distance, the ETA and why they were picked, and has 45 seconds to accept or decline. You can watch the packets travel from the request to each phone.

The best match among those who accept gets the job

The first accept opens a short window (4 seconds). When it closes, the technician with the highest match score among those who accepted wins: 38% closeness, 30% verified experience on this exact kind of fault (from the raw visit records), 12% skill, 20% availability. The others are told a better match took it. Try the scoring with any site and unit →

If everyone in the layer says no, the next layer opens

Declines and silence are written to the log. When nobody in a layer takes the job, the log says "Everyone within 3 km said no or did not answer. Widening the search to within 8 km" and the next ring lights up.

Busy technicians and the manager are the last resort

If every free technician has said no, the busy ones are asked, soonest first, with their remaining job time added to the ETA. If nobody at all can take it, the request turns red as Needs a manager.

The winner drives over, and the customer gets a one-time code

The job opens in their app. They can watch the four agents (Dispatcher, Diagnostician, Parts, Verifier) work in a Band room: what failed on this unit before, what to do first, what to bring. The reporter follows the drive on their own screen and sees a 4-digit OTP, shown only to them.

The OTP proves who is solving the problem

When the technician arrives, they ask the customer for the code and type it in. The work cannot start, and the job cannot be closed, until the right code is entered. A wrong code is logged, and after five wrong tries the job locks. Only then does the status become "Working".

While they work, the app becomes an assistant

When the work starts, the technician's app switches to a chat that already knows the unit. They can ask "what failed here before?", "what do I check first?" or "which part do I need?" and get answers from the shared memory, with the records behind them.

The visit is logged and the memory gets smarter

The technician logs what they tried, including what failed. The request closes, the technician is free again at the site, and the visit goes into the shared memory. The next match and the next briefing both use it.

What if…

The cases that decide whether a dispatch system is any good. Each one is a rule in the code, with a test.

Nobody is close

No free technician within 3 km, or 8, or 15

The layers with nobody free are skipped, each skip is logged, and the first notification goes to the nearest layer that has anyone free.

Try it: report a problem at Nexa Data Center (Electronic City). Nobody is within 8 km, so layer 1 is the 15 km ring.
The nearest has never seen it

A close technician with no history on this fault

Distance is 38% of the score, not 100%. If both accept, the technician with proven experience on this exact fault wins the job.

Try it: M-42 (cassette, MG Road). Farhan is 5.9 km away but has fixed 5 similar faults, so he beats the closer Arjun.
Two accept at once

Several technicians say yes

The first accept starts a 4-second window. When it ends, the highest match score among the acceptors wins; the rest are told a better match took it.

Everyone says no

The whole layer declines or stays silent

After everyone has answered, or 45 seconds pass, the next layer opens and every technician in it is notified. A technician is never asked twice for the same request.

A technician is offline

They switched to Offline, or are off shift

They are not notified and not asked as a last resort, and they show grey on the map. They cannot go offline in the middle of a job.

A wrong OTP

Someone enters a code that is not right

The attempt is logged, the work does not start, and after five wrong tries the job locks and the manager is told.

Everyone is busy

All free technicians have said no, or there are none

The busy ones are asked, soonest first, with their remaining job time added to the ETA. If even they cannot, the request escalates to a manager.

A new technician joins

No history yet

They are scored on distance, skill and availability only, with zero experience points. Every visit they log adds to the memory, so their score on that kind of fault rises with the work.

What is simulated. Coordinates, technician positions and availability are simulated (a fictional Bengaluru contractor, no real GPS). Technicians whose phone is not connected answer by themselves after a few seconds, so the screen is alive without phones. The match weights (38 / 30 / 12 / 20), the radii (3 / 8 / 15 / 30 km) and the 4-second window are set by hand, not learned, and are one line each to change in backend/dispatch.py. The repair histories behind the experience score are the sample company's synthetic records.
