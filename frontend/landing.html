The problem

Documents store information. They don't keep experience.

Manuals say how things should work. They rarely say what actually failed, what was tried first, and why the second attempt was the one that held.

01

People leave, knowledge leaves

The engineer who knew why that unit trips every summer retires. The next person starts from zero and repeats a costly diagnosis.

02

Storage is not recall

A shared drive holds thousands of files. Finding the one incident that matches today's symptom takes the very experience you've lost.

03

Failures vanish first

Reports record what worked. The approaches that failed, which save the most time, are almost never written down.

How it works

From one incident to everyone's knowledge.

1

Capture

Technicians and engineers log incidents: cause, what was tried, what failed, what finally worked.

2

Retain

Each experience is stored in long-term memory with its equipment, date and author, so its source is never lost.

3

Recall

Ask a question in plain language. Related incidents come back, including the ones that failed.

4

Verify & learn

Answers are checked against the records, and feedback corrects the memory for the next person.

The memory layer

Powered by Hindsight.

We don't rebuild memory. SiteMemory uses Hindsight's three operations, and each answer shows which records it drew on.

retain
Remember

Store an experience with its tags and source. It is processed in the background, so recall can lag by moments right after a save.

retain("Condenser fan capacitor failed…", tags=["HVAC-22"])
recall
Find

Search the bank for the records that match the question, scoped by tag when a machine or site is known.

recall("chiller trips on high head pressure")
→ records + sources
reflect
Reason

Write an answer from what was recalled, so the reply cites real history rather than guessing.

reflect(question)
→ answer grounded in the records
